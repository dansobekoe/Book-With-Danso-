Services and training

Research that holds up

Four kinds of research and four ways to learn. Every engagement starts with a conversation about what you already know.

Research

Four kinds of research

Each of these can stand alone, and many families end up using two or three together. The written record is the same in every case: sourced, dated where possible, and yours to keep.

Family tree research

Structured lineage research and verification. We work through the branches you want to follow using family testimony, records held in Ghana and archival sources, and we keep a note of the source behind each fact.

What it covers
Names, dates, places, marriages and burials, followed branch by branch and checked against records where records exist.
Who it is for
Families with some names and a town, who want the line set out properly and kept.
Ask about this service

Oral history planning

Guidance for African-centred family interviews. I help you choose which elders to speak with first, draft questions that open memory without causing harm, and plan how the recording will be stored and shared.

What it covers
Planning the interview, consent, questions, recording equipment, and the safe keeping of the files afterwards.
Who it is for
Families whose knowledge lives with elders and has not been written down.
Ask about this service

Grave documentation

Locating and documenting burial sites, respectfully. The record includes the location, photographs, the names and dates that survive on the marker, and the family's own account of who rests there.

What it covers
The marker, the plot, the surrounding land, and a written note of the family's account.
Who it is for
Families who want a record of where their people are buried, before the details are lost.
Ask about this service

Local heritage guidance

On the ground support for reconnecting with family and traditional ceremonies. This can include introductions to relatives, help with travel and timing, and guidance on the protocol expected when visiting family land.

What it covers
Introductions, travel and timing, and the customs to observe when you arrive.
Who it is for
Families returning to Ghana, or planning a first visit.

How the work is done

The standards behind every search

These apply whether the work is a single branch or a family reunion spread over years.

Evidence first

A name is only added when a record or a reliable interview supports it. Where the evidence is thin, the account says so.

The family stays in charge

You decide which branches to research and who may be contacted. Nothing is shared without the family's agreement.

Ghana wide

Accra is the base. The work goes wherever the family is, and online sessions reach families abroad.

Records kept

Notes, photographs and sources are organised and handed to you, so the record can be added to later.

Fees

Every family search is different, so fees are quoted after the first conversation, once the scope is clear. Coaching and workshops are quoted per session or per group.

You will always know the cost before any work begins.
